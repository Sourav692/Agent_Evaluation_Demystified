The one behaviour being scored above all others

Treat evaluation as a design input, not a phase that happens after the build. If you cannot describe how you would measure a component, you have not finished designing it. Every stage below serves that one sentence.

00

Get the answer skeleton

30 min

Before any content, get the reusable shape of an answer. Section 1 of the case-study bank is the Decomposition Spine — an eight-step flow from clarifying the ask to designing the feedback loop, deliberately eval-first. It is the backbone for every case in the document, and the single highest-leverage thing to memorise.

Two properties to say out loud when you draw the Spine

Online discovers; offline prevents recurrence. A finding that stops at the monitoring dashboard helps once. Carried into a gated offline test, it protects every release after.

The gate is the only thing that can deploy. Human-written and machine-optimised prompts pass the identical check — if anything the machine-written one deserves more scrutiny, because it was optimised against a metric sitting inside the gate.

Done when: you can draw the eight steps from memory and say what happens at each.

01

The taxonomy you must be able to draw

2–3 h

Almost every follow-up in this round is a disguised version of “which layer are you actually measuring?” You need the five surfaces — retrieval, generation, tool mechanics, trajectory, outcome — with at least one metric and one failure mode each, and you need the reference-based vs referenceless axis, because it decides everything operational downstream.

The four things to carry forward

1. The five surfaces, in order, with end-state verification as the strongest signal available. 2. Reference-based gates the deploy; referenceless watches what happens after it. 3. The scorer cost ladder — deterministic → wrapped judge → custom judge → trace-based judge. 4. The four judge failure modes: persuaded by fluency, rubric-sensitive, non-deterministic, circular when it is also the optimisation target.

Method matters more than coverage

Read the question, answer it out loud or on paper, then read the answer. If you read answers first you are training recognition, and this round tests recall under pressure. The self-quiz drill enforces this — answers stay hidden until you reveal them, and you rate your own recall so a second pass targets only the gaps.

05

The numbers, last and repeatedly

1 h, then re-skim

Specific numbers are what make an answer sound operated rather than read. Do this stage last, then re-skim it the morning of the interview.

The four numbers to have cold

95% per turn is 77% over five turns — and more likely failed than not by turn 14.
A 5% sample of 2,000 traces/day resolves to ±4.5 points — so a 2-point alert fires on noise; catching 2 points needs ≈606 scored traces, or 30% sampling.
Regression tolerance is 0.00 deterministic, 0.02 judged — the same −0.015 delta is tolerated on one and blocks on the other.
Two judges, identical exact agreement and identical plain kappa, scored +0.71 and −0.82 under quadratic weighted kappa.

Readiness check

do it cold

You are ready when you can do all six on a blank whiteboard, without notes.

The throughline, if you remember one thing

Task success alone is never enough, and aggregates hide the thing you need to see. A trace can reach the right final answer while being inefficient, unsafe, or lucky rather than reliable. A dashboard can be green while an entire attack class is at zero, an entire customer segment is failing, or the monitor itself stopped running three weeks ago. Say that out loud in the round, then show the decomposition that would have caught it.
